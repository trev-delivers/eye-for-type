# Sources

All read on 2026-10-18. Each line says what the source supports in this
skill.

## Critique practice

- **Design Critiques: Encourage a Positive Culture to Improve Products.**
  Sarah Gibbons, Nielsen Norman Group, 2016.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-critiques/
  Critique as analysing a design against its objectives to improve it;
  agree goals and the user problem first; scope the crit; avoid rushing to
  solve.
- **What Goes into a Well-Done Critique.** Jared M. Spool, Center Centre,
  2018. https://articles.centercentre.com/?p=173
  The 2 questions ("What is it we're really trying to do here?", "How
  close are we to doing it?"); critique versus proofing; justify praise and
  concerns with specifics; no hollow compliments; rank the most important
  point first; about 2 positives per negative; critique differs from
  heuristic evaluation and usability testing.
- **Discussing Design: Improving Communication and Collaboration through
  Critique.** Adam Connor and Aaron Irizarry, O'Reilly, 2015.
  https://oreilly.com/library/view/discussing-design/9781491902394
  Critique compares a design with its objectives. The book itself wasn't
  read; its framework is taken from the sources below, which agree.
- **Improve Your Designs Using Critique.** UIE, 2018.
  https://uxi.uie.com/blog/improve-your-designs-using-critique
  Connor and Irizarry's questions: objective, the elements chosen to meet
  it, whether they're effective, and why.
- **Adam Connor and Aaron Irizarry: Building Consensus in Critiques and
  Design Studios.** UIE Brain Sparks podcast, 2013.
  https://archive.uie.com/brainsparks/2013/08/06/adam-connor-aaron-irizarry-building-consensus-in-critiques-and-design-studios/
  Connor in his own words: critique is analysis comparing a design with
  how it meets its objectives, and "I don't like blue" isn't critique.
- Reaction, direction and critique as Connor and Irizarry's 3 kinds of
  feedback: found in several secondary summaries of the book (search on
  2026-10-18), not verified in the book's text. Kept because the summaries
  agree and the distinction is only used as a writing test.

## Heuristics and severity

- **10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design.** Jakob Nielsen,
  Nielsen Norman Group, 1994, reviewed 30 January 2024.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/
  The 10 heuristic names used as the vocabulary for "why it matters".
- **Severity Ratings for Usability Problems.** Jakob Nielsen, Nielsen
  Norman Group, 1994.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-rate-the-severity-of-usability-problems/
  The 0 to 4 scale and its names; frequency, impact, persistence and
  market impact.
- **Heuristic Evaluations: How to Conduct.** Kate Moran and Kelley Gordon,
  Nielsen Norman Group, 2023.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/
  2 passes (learn, then evaluate); 3 to 5 independent evaluators; a broken
  heuristic isn't automatically a problem (the hamburger menu example);
  heuristic evaluation can't replace user research.

## Perception and interaction

- **Visual Hierarchy in UX: Definition.** Kelley Gordon, Nielsen Norman
  Group, 2021.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/
  Colour and contrast, scale and grouping as the levers; the squint test;
  no more than 3 type sizes and 3 contrast levels in complex designs; no
  more than 2 elements at the largest size; headings closer to their
  content.
- **Text Scanning Patterns: Eyetracking Evidence.** Kara Pernice, Nielsen
  Norman Group, 2019.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-scanning-patterns-eyetracking/
  F-pattern, spotted, layer-cake and commitment patterns; descriptive
  headings and chunking help scanning.
- **Proximity Principle in Visual Design.** Aurora Harley, Nielsen Norman
  Group, 2020. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-proximity/
  Close things read as related; groupings can break when columns stack on
  small screens.
- **Similarity Principle in Visual Design.** Aurora Harley, Nielsen Norman
  Group, 2020. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-similarity/
  Same-looking buttons read as equally important; keep the link colour for
  things that can be clicked.
- **The Principle of Common Region: Containers Create Groupings.** Aurora Harley, Nielsen Norman Group,
  2020. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/common-region/
  Containers group; overuse adds clutter; full-width blocks can look like
  the end of the page ("false floors").
- **Fitts's Law and Its Applications in UX.** Raluca Budiu, Nielsen Norman
  Group, 2022. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/fitts-law/
  Bigger and nearer targets are faster; labels enlarge icon targets;
  crowded small targets cause wrong taps; put the submit button near the
  last field.
- **Hick's Law.** Laws of UX (Jon Yablonski).
  https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/
  Decision time grows with the number and complexity of choices; break
  complex tasks into steps; highlight recommended options; don't simplify
  to the point of abstraction.
- **Response Time Limits.** Jakob Nielsen, Nielsen
  Norman Group, 1993, updated 2014.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/
  0.1s feels instant, 1s keeps flow, 10s loses attention; the basis for
  "feedback within about 100ms".
- **Confirmation Dialogs Can Prevent User Errors.** Jakob
  Nielsen, Nielsen Norman Group, 2018, reviewed 2026.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/
  Routine confirmations get clicked through; keep them for rare, serious
  actions; provide undo.

## States

- **Designing Empty States in Complex Applications: 3 Guidelines.** Kate
  Kaplan, Nielsen Norman Group, 2021.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/
  Say what's happening (don't show "no records" while loading), teach in
  place, offer a direct path to the task.
- **Skeleton Screens 101.** Samhita Tankala, Nielsen Norman Group, 2023,
  reviewed 2026. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/
  Nothing under 1s; skeletons for full-page loads under 10s; spinners for
  one component; progress bar with an estimate past 10s.
- **Error-Message Guidelines.** Tim Neusesser and Evan Sunwall, Nielsen
  Norman Group, 2023.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/
  Message next to the cause, plain words, constructive advice, keep the
  user's input, don't rely on colour alone.

## Motion

- **Executing UX Animations: Duration and Motion Characteristics.** Page
  Laubheimer, Nielsen Norman Group, 2020.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/
  About 100ms for simple feedback, 200 to 300ms for bigger changes, 400ms
  only for large moves; too long is more common than too short; entrances
  slightly longer than exits; ease-out in, ease-in out; frequent
  animations shorter; honour reduced motion.

## Accessibility and platforms

- **What's New in WCAG 2.2.** W3C WAI, updated 5 October 2023.
  https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/
  WCAG 2.2 is the current Recommendation; new criteria include 2.4.11
  Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) and 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum); 4.1.1
  Parsing removed.
- **Understanding SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum).** W3C WAI.
  https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html
  4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text (18pt, or 14pt bold, roughly 24px and
  18.66px); ratios aren't rounded; disabled controls exempt. The capture
  script's contrast check uses these numbers.
- **Understanding SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum).** W3C WAI.
  https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html
  24 by 24 CSS px at AA, with spacing and inline exceptions. The capture
  script's "under 24px" count and its inline-link exemption.
- **Understanding SC 2.5.5 Target Size (Enhanced).** W3C WAI.
  https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html
  44 by 44 CSS px at AAA, which matches obvious's `--ds-control-tap-min`.
- **Understanding SC 1.4.10 Reflow.** W3C WAI.
  https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html
  No 2-direction scrolling at 320 CSS px wide (1280px at 400% zoom).
- **Human Interface Guidelines: Accessibility.** Apple.
  https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility
  iOS and iPadOS default control size 44 by 44pt, minimum 28 by 28pt
  (read from the page's data file, as the page itself renders with
  JavaScript).
- **Touch target size.** Android Accessibility Help, Google.
  https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858
  At least 48 by 48dp, separated by 8dp, per Material's accessibility
  guidance. Material's own site (m3.material.io) wouldn't render for
  reading, so this is the citation.
